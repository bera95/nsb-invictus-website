**Short answer:** a good SaaS MVP solves *one* painful problem for *one* clearly defined customer, with secure accounts, the core workflow, basic administration, analytics and a way to get paid. Everything else waits until real users tell you what matters.

## Step 1 — Define the problem, not the feature list

Write one sentence: *“[Customer] struggles with [problem], which costs them [time/money]. Our product lets them [outcome].”* If you cannot write it, you are not ready to build.

## Step 2 — Cut the scope to one core workflow

List every feature, then mark each as *must have for the first paying customer* or *later*. Typical MVP must-haves:

- Sign-up, login, password reset (and optionally single sign-on)
- The core workflow that delivers the outcome
- A simple admin view
- Subscription billing or a pilot-payment flow
- Product analytics and error monitoring

## Step 3 — Choose an architecture that will not trap you

- **Multi-tenancy from day one** — every record belongs to an organisation.
- **A mainstream, well-supported stack** so hiring and maintenance stay easy.
- **Infrastructure as code and automated deployments** so releases are routine.
- **Clear API boundaries** for future integrations and mobile apps.

## Step 4 — Build in short, visible iterations

Ship working software every one or two weeks to a small group of design partners. Weekly feedback beats months of guessing.

## Step 5 — Launch, measure, decide

Measure activation (did users reach the outcome?), retention (did they come back?) and willingness to pay. These three numbers decide what you build next.

## The most expensive mistakes

1. **Building for everyone** instead of one specific customer.
2. **Gold-plating** — perfect settings pages before anyone uses the core feature.
3. **Ignoring billing** until late, then discovering pricing does not work.
4. **No analytics**, so decisions are based on opinions.
5. **Throwaway code** that has to be rewritten the moment the product succeeds.

## After the MVP

Once usage is proven, invest in onboarding, integrations, performance, security reviews and — increasingly — AI features that automate the work your users still do by hand.
