**Short answer:** to rank in several countries, give each market its own localised section on one domain, connect versions with hreflang, build genuine local signals (Google Business Profile where you have a presence, local citations, local content) and measure each market separately in Search Console.

## Choose the right structure

- **Subfolders on one domain** (`/de/`, `/fr/`, `/en-us/`) — the best default for most businesses.
- **Country domains** (`.de`, `.ca`) — strong local signal, but authority is split and costs multiply.
- **Subdomains** — workable, but usually no advantage over subfolders.

## Localise, do not just translate

Each market version should reflect local currency, spelling, units, regulations, examples and contact options. A page written for Germany should not read like a translated US page.

## Implement hreflang correctly

- Add hreflang for every language/region pair, including a self-reference.
- Add `x-default` for visitors who match no version.
- Make sure every version links back to the others (return tags).
- Keep canonical tags pointing to each page itself, not to another language.

## Build local signals

- **Google Business Profile** for every real location or service area.
- **Consistent name, address and phone** across directories.
- **Local citations and partnerships** — chambers of commerce, industry associations, local media.
- **Reviews** from customers in each market.

## Create market-specific content

City and region pages work when they contain genuinely local information: projects, regulations, pricing context, local FAQs. Thin pages that only swap the city name tend to be ignored.

## Measure per market

Use Search Console country filters and GA4 regional reports to track impressions, clicks and conversions in each market, and adjust content and links accordingly.
